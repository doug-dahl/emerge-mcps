## Cutting it in

A b-roll clip has nothing spoken in it, so a `stitch_clips` part omits
`transcript_file_id` entirely and addresses footage by time:

```python
parts=[
  { "clip_file_id": "<interview mp4>", "transcript_file_id": "<txt>",
    "keep_segments": [12, 13], "label": "freedom line" },
  { "clip_file_id": "1NfulWN_7O0A7eiNCZUkphudR7jc1E9XF",   # hanging-off-the-truck, 9:16
    "keep_ranges": [{"start": "00:02.0", "end": "00:07.0"}],
    "volume": 0.2, "label": "b-roll: hanging off the truck" },
]
```

- **No transcript ⇒ `keep_ranges` is required** (there are no segment indices to
  reference) and the part contributes no captions.
- **`volume` ducks the natural sound.** These clips carry live yard ambience —
  engines, wind, chatter — recorded at full level. Left alone it jumps out
  against interview audio. Use `0.15`–`0.3` for presence, `0` to mute.
- **`pad` defaults to False** for a b-roll part, so you get exactly the range you
  asked for (padding only exists to protect the first and last word of speech).
- Ranges are relative to the clip's own start, and these clips are 2–10s long —
  ask for a window inside the duration in the table above.

**Keep the voice running over the picture.** Doug's standing note from the
Raheine cuts: *"I'd rather have words over the silent b-roll to ease
transitions."* Silent b-roll breathers read as dead air. `stitch_clips`
concatenates parts, so a b-roll part inserted between two speech parts pauses the
narration for its duration — keep those inserts to ~2–3s, or place them where a
sentence has genuinely finished. A true L-cut (voice from the previous part
continuing under the picture) is not native to `stitch_clips`; that needs a local
ffmpeg render.
